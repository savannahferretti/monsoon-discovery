\subsection{Prediction Framework}

Because precipitation spans several orders of magnitude and predictors differ
in units and scale, all models operate on standardized inputs and a transformed
precipitation target. Each predictor is standardized to zero mean and unit
variance using its training-set mean ($\mu$) and standard deviation ($s$),
except LF, which is not standardized because it is time-invariant and bounded
within $[0,\,1]$. For vertically varying predictors, standardization is applied
independently at each grid point before kernel integration (Section~3.3).
Because the training-set mean and standard deviation are computed globally
across all grid points and vertical levels, the kernel-integrated standardized
feature equals the standardized form of the corresponding kernel-integrated
physical feature (Text~SX).

The precipitation target is transformed in two steps. First, we apply $y =
\log(1 + P)$, where $P$ is precipitation in mm, to compress the heavy-tailed
distribution. Second, we standardize $y$ to zero mean and unit variance using
the training-set statistics $\mu_y$ and $s_y$. Each model is then trained to
minimize the mean squared error between its output and the standardized $y$.

All architectures enforce non-negative predictions through the same mechanism.
Each model learns a function $f(\mathbf{x})$ of the standardized predictors and
produces the standardized log-precipitation prediction
%
\begin{equation}
  z \;=\; z_{\min} + \max\!\bigl(f(\mathbf{x}),\;0\bigr),
  \qquad
  z_{\min} = -\,\frac{\mu_y}{s_y}\,,
  \label{eq:zfloor}
\end{equation}
%
where $z_{\min}$ is the standardized value corresponding to zero physical
precipitation. Because $\max(f(\mathbf{x}),\,0) \geq 0$, the prediction
$z \geq z_{\min}$, and physical precipitation is guaranteed non-negative after
denormalization.

Physical precipitation is recovered by inverting the standardization and
log-transformation. Substituting Equation~\ref{eq:zfloor} into $P =
\exp(\mu_y + s_y\,z) - 1$ and simplifying, $\mu_y$ cancels
(Text~SX in Supporting Information~S1) and the prediction reduces to
%
\begin{equation}
  P \;=\; \max\!\Bigl(\exp\!\bigl(s_y\,f(\mathbf{x})\bigr) - 1,\;\;0\Bigr).
  \label{eq:pred}
\end{equation}
%
Given any model $f(\mathbf{x})$ and its fitted constants,
Equation~\ref{eq:pred} together with the per-predictor standardization
statistics are sufficient to convert predictions to physical precipitation in
mm. The full derivation of Equation~\ref{eq:pred} and the physical-space forms
of each symbolic regression equation are provided in Text~SX and Table~SX of
Supporting Information~S1.
